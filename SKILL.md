# IMC Art Direction

This companion supplies IMC's creative and integration requirements. The installed `sprite-gen` skill supplies generation workflow, extraction, assembly, previews and export. Keep upstream tooling separate; do not fork or edit it just to change an art brief.

## Establish the task

Use the current request and project context to identify the character/location, requested output and whether the work is documentation, concept generation, asset production or game integration. Carry forward stated choices. Do not generate images during a documentation-only task. A request to generate a candidate does not by itself require replacing production textures or editing gameplay.

Read [references/project-contract.md](references/project-contract.md) to locate the active IMC documents and relevant asset bindings. Resolve paths against the actual IMC checkout, including worktrees; the original local checkout is `D:/Godot Projects/IMC-godot`. Read current sources rather than treating this skill's implementation snapshot as permanent. Inspect approved images when doing visual work; their existence or a historical audit does not prove current visual approval.

Proceed with reasonable, recorded choices when the brief and references resolve them. Ask only when a missing identity reference, conflicting design source or unspecified purpose would materially change the result. Do not make users re-answer style preferences, choose individual scripts or approve every routine step.

## Shared visual direction

- Preserve IMC's Japanese illustrated fantasy / Unicorn-Overlord-inspired character direction. Backgrounds follow the user's PawPop 2 rendering reference: crisp 2D-HD/chibi JRPG environments with clean anime linework, controlled cel-shading and minimal painterly texture, as defined below. Portraits use detailed character illustration; Commander, Officer, recruit and humanoid NPC gameplay sprites use the full-body chibi anime style defined by the user-selected visual reference. Before sprite work, read the project's `docs/visual/gameplay-sprite-style.md` and inspect `docs/visual/references/gameplay-sprite-style-2026-09-13.jpeg`. Match its actual proportions and rendering; do not substitute an older atlas, a rejected candidate, an invented head count or generic "big head, short limbs" wording. Avoid Western game-art styling, photorealism, gritty realism and generic mobile-anime rendering.
- Keep texture restrained. Use broad readable material shapes, selective highlights and detail concentrated around meaningful focal points. Avoid blanket grain, noisy brush texture, excessive surface scratches, embossed clutter and random filigree. Preserve necessary material distinction; restrained texture does not mean flat or unfinished art.
- Clean contours are crucial for both portraits and gameplay sprites. Use continuous, controlled edges with slightly stronger outline weight than the earlier thin Elsie treatment, while retaining fine interior detail. Avoid jagged speckles, broken/doubled lines and dark or bright cutout fringes; thickening an unclean edge does not fix it. Read [references/rendering-contract.md](references/rendering-contract.md) for contour, background and compositing review.
- Generate no unrequested text, pseudo-writing, signatures, labels, watermarks or UI. Keep game labels and controls in their existing runtime layer. An explicitly requested inscription is a separate intentional element, not permission to fill surfaces with gibberish.
- Match camera angle, lighting direction, palette and intended display scale to the approved asset family. Review at gameplay size as well as close-up; decorative detail must survive downscaling without obscuring the subject.
- Maintain frame-to-frame color and lighting consistency using the same approved appearance reference throughout an animation. Slight shading variation is acceptable, as in the user's accepted Elsie animation; obvious complexion changes, material-color jumps or distracting lighting flicker are not. Follow the practical tolerance and material-specific review in [references/rendering-contract.md](references/rendering-contract.md#frame-to-frame-color-and-lighting-consistency); do not pursue pixel-identical shading or equal whole-frame color averages.
- When integrating approved artwork, keep display sizing in runtime layout data: world actors use a per-character standing height with uniform X/Y scaling, and portraits use aspect-preserving fit boxes. Never compensate for a small or differently shaped source by stretching one axis or rewriting the source asset.

## Characters and uploaded references

Existing named characters retain their approved identity. Preserve recognizable face/eye and hair design, age presentation, relative body build, costume structure/colors, equipment and signature features. Translate portrait anatomy into the approved gameplay sprite proportions; identity continuity does not require identical anatomical ratios across portrait and sprite. Lock the approved sprite's own proportions across its directions and motion. Role titles are not body-design instructions.

Female characters need deliberate appeal and distinct personalities rather than interchangeable faces and bodies. Develop appeal through expression, posture, silhouette, costume and individual body concept. Elegant, mature, glamorous, athletic, soft/chubby and petite adult designs can all belong to the cast. Do not automatically maximize exposed skin, slim every woman or make adults childlike. Other important characters also need deliberate, memorable appeal. Valerie specifically remains a mature, clearly chubbier, warm and confident merchant; retain that body concept in full-body animation as well as portraits.

For a reference-based request:

1. Inspect each supplied image and record its role: identity, rendering style, costume, pose, composition or motion. When the user says "make this character," default to preserving that character's identity while applying IMC rendering. Do not copy an unrelated style reference's face or a pose reference's costume.
2. Separate features to preserve from explicitly requested changes. For an existing IMC character, resolve a consequential conflict between an uploaded reference and the approved identity before producing a replacement. For a new character, develop the supplied design without inventing a game role, recruitment rule or canonical approval.
3. Save a concise character brief with the production run: stable ID, reference paths/revisions and their roles, design locks, requested changes, appeal, target output/scale, selected source and QA status. A candidate remains a candidate until selected or approved; record which reference later work should reuse.
4. For Officer production, follow the user's separate stages: approved portrait, then a separately requested single full-body gameplay sprite for approval, then required animations after sprite approval and a request to continue. An already approved portrait is reused. Attach the portrait for identity and the user-selected sprite sheet for proportions/rendering; do not use either as a substitute for the other. A portrait alone does not establish unseen costume or rear equipment details; establish missing views as proposed designs. Use an already approved gameplay sprite directly for later animation requests.

Preserve cast diversity and asymmetrical features. Do not mirror a character with asymmetric clothing, hair or equipment as if the missing direction had been authored and verified.

## Sprites and animation

Read [references/animation-contract.md](references/animation-contract.md) for timing, current compatibility and motion QA. The user's current IMC production rule is exactly **16 frames for every new animation**, unless a later explicit request overrides it. Do not replace this with sprite-gen's demonstration default, historical frame ranges, or repeated filler poses. More frames alone do not establish smooth or correct animation.

New complete Officer animation sets include idle, work, greeting/interaction and four-direction walking: Up, Down, Left, Right. All newly produced walk/run/jog/sprint sets use only those four directions unless the user explicitly changes the requirement. Follow the animation contract's per-frame anatomical leg plan, alternating contact and loop-seam review; a valid 16-frame export alone does not pass motion QA. Work/greeting must arrive at a natural resting pose on frame 8 for a deliberate hold, then conclude through frames 9-16. Adult female sprite animation includes clothing-aware, localized bust secondary motion in every action with movement; verify that motion against a stable torso and garment rather than accepting a prompt mention as evidence. Jiggle does not mean adding whole-body sway, rocking or bouncing. Elsie's existing tight-leather treatment is an accepted exception. Every new 16-frame character animation must contain exactly one complete eye blink, with eyes open during deliberate holds; follow the contract for blink planning and hidden-eye views. These requirements do not turn a single-sprite or documentation request into animation production or authorize runtime roaming.

Use sprite-gen's installed `SKILL.md` and only the route documents needed for the requested operation. On this installation, use its absolute `run-sprite-gen.cmd` launcher. Follow active tool instructions for image generation/editing; this companion does not authorize an alternate provider or bypass provider selection. Keep provider defaults separate from IMC art direction and preserve choices already made by the user.

For IMC's illustrated rendering, disable pixel-unfake (`fit.pixel_unfake: false`) and avoid palette quantization or pixel-grid styling unless the user explicitly requests a pixel-art variant. Choose chroma against the actual costume/hair colors, then verify alpha and edges. Use the canonical row extraction and composition workflow; historical fixed-grid prompts describe old deliveries, not a reason to generate one crowded grid or slice new poses blindly.

Keep each production run and its candidate revisions separate from live game assets. Export the selected/curated result through the pipeline, not its uncurated frame cache. Do not claim that a generated manifest is directly compatible with IMC's custom renderer without checking it.

## Backgrounds, maps and monsters

Before background production, read the background section of [references/rendering-contract.md](references/rendering-contract.md#background-direction-pawpop-2) and inspect its retained PawPop 2 image. Use its crisp anime contours, simplified material planes, controlled cel-shading, medium-detail floor surfaces and graphic foliage; reserve atmospheric softness for distant layers. This supersedes the earlier broadly painterly background instruction. Apply the rendering approach to IMC's location and mood; the reference's shrine architecture, night palette and pasted characters are not universal design requirements.

For a new or redesigned background, develop an original composition appropriate to the department/location; the current background is not a mandatory design or image-generation reference. Read environment/map documents and runtime geometry to identify functional needs: room ownership, connections, traversable space, actor staging, perspective and UI space. Lock existing image coordinates only for an explicitly requested layout-preserving replacement. A redesign may propose new staging, entrances and occlusion geometry; record required runtime adjustments before integration instead of forcing the design around an old desk crop. Keep gameplay actors and their contact shadows separate from the empty background. Review with approved sprites at actual gameplay scale. Do not bake in extra people, labels or controls, or assume the game supports unimplemented parallax layers.

For monsters, preserve the species, anatomy, silhouette and catalog role. Character-appeal guidance does not turn existing creatures into humanoids or authorize new recruitable species.

## Review and delivery

Review identity and appeal, rendering restraint, silhouette at intended size and technical output separately. Compare a standing sprite candidate with the user-selected sprite reference at comparable visible height, checking head/torso balance, limbs and boot scale; a style label in a prompt is not evidence of a match. A technically valid but generic or inconsistent character needs revision. For animation, examine playback and contact sheets, not just the atlas. Report missing views, identity drift, bad contacts, texture flicker, duplicate filler poses or failed alpha checks explicitly; never call unreviewed output production-ready.

Deliver the requested candidate or export with a short account of what changed, relevant file paths, checks actually performed and any integration work still needed. For documentation/skill edits, list every authored file and its purpose. Do not silently update project documents, production assets or game code as a side effect of maintaining this companion.
